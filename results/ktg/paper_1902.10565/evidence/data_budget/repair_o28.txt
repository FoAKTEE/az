Root cause: scratch_guard.sh took ONE sample of `du -sb`, and du exits non-zero when
an entry disappears from under its walk - routine on a live mission root that other
jobs are writing. A healthy cycle was therefore aborted at exit 3 ('cannot measure').
An earlier section-2 run in this same repair had returned exit 1 for the same reason;
at that moment the cause was not yet identified and was recorded as unattributed.

Fix: retry a non-zero du (KTG_DU_ATTEMPTS, default 3, KTG_DU_RETRY_SLEEP 2 s apart) in
both scratch_guard.sh and prune_retention.py rolling mode. The measurement itself is
NOT softened: a non-zero du is discarded even when it printed a partial total, because
a partial total UNDER-counts - an unreadable subtree would otherwise hide usage from
the cap - and a root that keeps failing still exits 3, exactly as before. Contract
cases T1 (readable control, exits 1 on the cap) and T2 (unreadable subdirectory: du
fails on all three attempts and does print a partial total; the guard discards it and
exits 3) pin both halves.

Also hardened while here: a killed test run used to leave a raised-cap probe file
(loose_probe.<pid>.env) inside the guard's own allow-listed directory, where a later
caller could have pointed KTG_BUDGET_ENV at it. run_guard_tests.sh now sweeps those
at startup as well as on exit.
